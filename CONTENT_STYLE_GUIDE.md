# Content Style Guide

## 1. Japanese writing

Prefer natural modern Japanese.

Avoid:
- unnatural textbook Japanese,
- literal translations,
- overly stiff phrases unless the register specifically requires it.

---

# 2. Translation

Every important medical entry should ideally contain:

- Japanese
- kana
- English
- Indonesian

Romaji should be optional and disabled by default for advanced learners.

---

# 3. Furigana

Do not show furigana permanently.

Use:
- hover,
- click,
- toggle.

The user should practice reading kanji directly.

---

# 4. Clinical phrases

For each important intent, include:

- standard polite phrase,
- simpler patient-friendly alternative,
- notes on nuance.

Example:

Standard:
どのような痛みですか。

Simpler:
どんな痛みですか。

---

# 5. Disease explanations

Patient explanations should avoid unnecessarily technical vocabulary.

Bad:
冠動脈の急性閉塞による心筋壊死です。

Better patient explanation:
心臓に血液を送る血管が詰まり、心臓の筋肉にダメージが起きる病気です。

---

# 6. Reading explanations

Do not only say:
"Option B is correct."

Explain:
- why B is correct,
- which sentence supports it,
- why the other options are wrong,
- what paraphrase was used,
- what reading skill was tested.

---

# 7. Grammar explanations

Always include:
- meaning,
- nuance,
- construction,
- register,
- comparison with similar grammar,
- at least 3 examples.

---

# 8. Difficulty

Content should not be simplified excessively.

The target user is already intermediate.

Reading materials should progressively expose:
- abstract vocabulary,
- formal syntax,
- long sentences,
- inference.

---

# 9. Medical safety

Do not write clinical content as patient-specific medical advice.

Use educational phrasing.

Where local medical practice varies, avoid claiming one universal protocol.

---

# 10. Data quality

Every medical translation should be reviewed for:
- clinical meaning,
- natural Japanese,
- patient appropriateness,
- ambiguity.

Flag uncertain content rather than inventing certainty.
